# Phase 4 findings: exploratory analysis of the SIMULATED delay data

**All numbers below come from the SIMULATED medium scenario (the delay simulator on the Corridor B timetable). None describe real Indian Railways delays.** Figures are in `reports/figures/` (`eda_NN_*.png`, each titled "[SIMULATED]"); the analysis is `notebooks/05_eda.ipynb`, helpers in `src/analysis/eda.py`.

## Data used

- **Decision window: 2024-01-01 .. 2024-10-31** (fold 1's training range): 4.64 M arrivals, 68,625 train runs, 305 days. Every number below that informs modelling comes from this window only.
- **Descriptive only, not used for decisions:** the monthly series in `eda_06` (Nov 2024 - Aug 2025) and the fog scoring of the baselines on Nov-Dec 2024 (`eda_15`, right column). Fold 1's validation data (Nov-Dec 2024) therefore influenced no modelling decision.
- Never loaded: validation fold 2 (Sep-Oct 2025), the test window (Nov-Dec 2025), `groundtruth_cascades*`, `delay_attribution`, the events table, the queue log, and the cause-label columns of the observation table (a test checks this).
- **Window note:** the simulated data covers calendar 2024 and 2025, so the training window is 2024-01-01 .. 2025-08-31 as configured; 2024 and 2025 share one timetable, so this is the same world twice with different weather and incidents.
- Only what real data would hold was used: arrival/departure times and delays, the weather state (as if from a weather source) and the timetable.
- Cautions that apply throughout: per-train results for 12003 and 12033 are not interpreted (known Shatabdi model artifact); every train runs daily in this world; one lane per direction, headway queueing and random freight shape what follows.

## Findings

### 1. Fog and season set the level of delay far more than anything else
In the decision window the mean arrival delay is **37.2 min on fog days (22 days) against 8.7 on clear days (231) and 13.8 on rain days (52)**; only 33% of fog-day arrivals are within 10 minutes against 78% on clear days (p90: 93 vs 27 min). Monthly means are 20.8 (Jan) and 22.1 (Feb, the fog months) against 7.1-11.3 for March-October. By hour, delay is highest for scheduled arrivals around 10:00 (17.0 min) and lowest around 17:00 (6.8 min). Day of week has only a 2.0-minute spread (Sunday and Monday highest, 12.8; Saturday 10.8). Along the chain, mean delay builds up in the direction of travel: for down trains from 0.3 min at New Delhi to about 20 min at Mughalsarai, for up trains from about 5 min at Mughalsarai to 25 min at New Delhi, with drops at Kanpur and Prayagraj where trains join or leave the corridor (a change in which trains are counted, not recovery).
*Figures:* `eda_05_weather`, `eda_03_hour_month_dow`, `eda_02_station_chain`; descriptive monthly view `eda_06_monthly_descriptive` (Dec 2024 and Jan 2025, 10 fog days each, average 20.4 and 20.6).
*SIMULATED caveat:* synthetic weather, with only 22 fog days in the window and a day-to-day persistence parameter (0.6) built into the weather generator.
*Likely artifact?* **Largely yes.** The size of the fog effect is a parameter choice (12% slowdown on run time, doubled incident probability, a 2.5x multiplier on entry delay, strongest from Delhi to Kanpur). The day-of-week spread is almost certainly noise or weather clustering: nothing in the simulator depends on the weekday and every train runs daily. The hour-of-day profile follows the (fixed daily) timetable and the entry delays of long-distance trains, and the build-up along the chain follows from finding 2 (delay is carried).

### 2. Delay is carried along a route almost one-for-one; the corridor gains and loses large amounts of delay but nets out near zero
Delay at the next stop of the same train correlates **0.996** with delay now (0.984 three stops on, 0.93 ten stops on, 0.68 forty stops on); the regression slope is 1.008 with a 0.1-minute intercept. Mean delay added across a section is -0.006 min, and only 1.5% of passages add more than a minute (0.5% lose more than a minute). Yet gross flows are large: sections gain about **1,930 delay-minutes per day and lose about 2,020**, halts recover a net 440 per day (-0.37 min per halt, 410 gained and 850 lost). Individual trains differ a lot: of six long trains, Rajdhani 22824 goes from 0.1 min late on entry to 50 min at the last stop, Garib Rath 12877 from 6 to 103, Duronto 12282 from 0.1 to 8, and Sikkim Mahananda 14083 from 7 to 6.
*Figures:* `eda_09_same_train_dependence`, `eda_07_routes`, `eda_08_gain_loss`.
*SIMULATED caveat:* the carrying and the gain/loss pattern depend on the simulator's recovery rule.
*Likely artifact?* **Substantially.** Recovery is a fixed 5-8% of scheduled section time plus shortened halts, delay is injected mainly as an entry delay drawn independently of the route, and trains cannot pass each other between stations. Real trains recover differently and real entry delays are not independent draws, so the near-perfect carrying (0.996) is probably too strong; the qualitative result (delay mostly persists along a route) is likely to hold.

### 3. A late train drags the next train at the same station; hourly station delay has hours-long memory
For consecutive arrivals of different trains at the same node and direction, the two delays correlate **0.80 when the arrivals are less than 2 minutes apart, 0.57 (2-5 min), 0.52 (5-10 min), 0.45 (20-30 min), 0.35 (1-2 h) and 0.14 (2-6 h)**; after removing each node's hour-of-day and monthly mean the values are 0.75, 0.50, 0.44, 0.36, 0.24 and 0.09, so most (not all) of the short-gap coupling is not just a shared time of day. 50% of consecutive arrivals at a node are within 10 minutes of each other. Hourly mean delay at a station has lag-1-hour autocorrelation 0.40-0.53 (0.71 at New Delhi), about 0.27 at 3 hours and 0.2-0.27 at 6-24 hours even after removing the hour-of-day mean.
*Figures:* `eda_10_different_trains`, `eda_11_station_acf`.
*SIMULATED caveat:* the timetable is old and dense, and all trains run daily.
*Likely artifact?* **Yes, in magnitude.** The 0.80 at short gaps is mostly mechanical: in the simulator a follower waits behind a leader (headway rule, one lane per direction), and daily running makes the corridor about twice as dense as a real one (half of consecutive arrivals within 10 minutes). Real data will have a smaller coupling; the longer-lag memory also reflects shared fog and entry-delay patterns.

### 4. A disturbed station stays disturbed for hours, and longer in fog season
A node and direction is "disturbed" from the first hour its mean arrival delay reaches 30 minutes until it falls below 15 (thresholds fixed beforehand; hours with no arrivals filled over up to 3 hours). With the first definition (A) the median episode lasts 2 hours in every season, but **36% of Jan-Feb episodes are still going after 3 hours against about 20% in March-October, and 9% after 12 hours against 0%**. Because a single late train can start an episode (there are only 3-4 arrivals per node, direction and hour), definition B requires at least 3 arrivals in the hour: the median becomes 4 hours in Jan-Feb (2-3 elsewhere), 51% are still disturbed after 3 hours in Jan-Feb (32-39% elsewhere), 32% after 6 hours (16-21%) and 17% after 12 hours (5-8%). 63,588 episodes under A, 37,257 under B; none are censored.
*Figure:* `eda_12_persistence`.
*SIMULATED caveat:* the sensitivity to the definition is large, and the numbers describe this world.
*Likely artifact?* **Partly.** Longer persistence in fog season comes from the built-in fog slowdown, which lowers the ability to recover (slack cannot be used) as well as raising delays; the absolute durations depend on headway queues, entry delays of through trains and the 30/15-minute thresholds. Treat the ordering fog season > other seasons as the robust part.

### 5. Delay is heavy-tailed and concentrated in a small share of runs
The **worst 1% of runs (686 of 68,625) account for 15.4% of all delay-minutes, the worst 5% for 42.4%, the worst 10% for 60.4% and the worst 20% for 80.1%**; by arrival rows the worst 1% carry 15.4% and the worst 5% carry 44.2%. 10.9% of runs have at least one arrival more than 60 minutes late and 3.1% more than 120. Days are less concentrated: the worst 5% of days carry 17.3% of delay-minutes and the worst 10% carry 29.1% (305 days). In fog months the worst 5% of runs carry less (33%) than in other seasons (42-47%) because fog delays many runs, not a few.
*Figure:* `eda_13_heavy_tails`.
*SIMULATED caveat:* the tail shape is built from the parameters.
*Likely artifact?* **Partly.** Incident durations are drawn from a log-normal with a Pareto tail (5%, capped at 240 minutes) and heavy mid-route entry delays, and cascades then multiply them; so a heavy tail is by construction. The concentration in a few runs follows from finding 2 (delay, once large, stays with the train). Real railways are also heavy-tailed, but the exact shares should not be transferred.

### 6. Differences between train types are mostly exposure; the Shatabdi class is an artifact
Raw mean delay: Rajdhani 9.3 min, Duronto 9.0, Shatabdi 17.7, Superfast 12.2, Mail/Express 11.3, Passenger 7.6. This is not ordered by priority because types differ in how much of the corridor they cross (107 rows per run for Rajdhani, 103 Duronto, 77 Superfast, 45 Mail/Express, 19 Passenger) and in how they enter (93% of Mail/Express trains enter mid-route with an unknown incoming delay: 7.1 min on the first row against 2.3 for Rajdhani; only 12% of Passenger trains do). The delay gained inside the corridor (last row minus first row) is larger for the long-distance priority trains (+19 Rajdhani, +21 Duronto) than for Mail/Express (+9) and Passenger (+5), again an exposure effect. Shatabdi's 17.7 minutes comes from two trains (12003, 12033); without them the Shatabdi mean is 3.9 minutes.
*Figure:* `eda_04_by_type`.
*SIMULATED caveat:* train types, priorities and the entry-delay draw are simulator inputs; per-train results for 12003 and 12033 were deliberately not interpreted.
*Likely artifact?* **Yes for the Shatabdi class** (known model artifact) **and partly for the rest** (priority yielding, one-lane no-passing rule and the entry-delay model decide who is delayed).

### 7. For 1-6 hour forecasts, delay upstream, hour-of-day history and fog look informative; scheduled congestion and short-term trend do not
Evidence on an hourly node-and-direction frame (not saved as a prediction table). Spearman correlation with the delay h hours later (h = 1 / 3 / 6): **upstream node three steps back 0.55 / 0.25 / 0.16** and one step back 0.44 / 0.24 / 0.16 (both higher than the node's own current delay, 0.39 / 0.24 / 0.17 at 1 hour), **hour-of-day history 0.48 at every horizon**, **fog 0.30**, rain 0.17, opposite direction at the same node 0.11 / 0.10 / 0.09, the one-hour trend 0.14 / 0.07 / -0.01, arrivals in the hour 0.12 / 0.12 / 0.02, and **scheduled trains per hour only 0.03**. In a linear model fitted on Jan-Jul and scored on Aug-Oct 2024 (R-squared at 1 / 3 / 6 hours): current delay 0.25 / 0.02 / -0.03; adding upstream and opposite-direction delay 0.45 / 0.04 / -0.03; adding hour-of-day history **0.57 / 0.39 / 0.35**; adding weather 0.58 / 0.42 / 0.39; adding scheduled congestion adds nothing. So candidates for later are: the current delay of the train and node, delay at upstream nodes (several steps), hour of day (via history or encoded), weather state (current and, if available, forecast), and fog flag interactions; scheduled congestion looked uninformative here, and the short-term trend only helps at 1 hour.
*Figure:* `eda_14_feature_evidence`.
*SIMULATED caveat:* linear sets are only evidence; they were fitted with fog months in the fit period and scored on a fog-free period, which shifts the intercepts (note the negative R-squared of current delay at 6 hours).
*Likely artifact?* **Partly.** The hour-of-day gain reflects a fixed daily timetable with every train running daily (a real timetable varies by weekday, so the pattern would weaken); upstream delay matters because trains run one way along one line and carry delay (finding 2); the weak scheduled-congestion result partly reflects a timetable that does not vary day to day, so its hourly pattern is already captured by hour of day.

### 8. What the models must beat: delay carried by the train itself is a very strong 1-2 hour baseline, and long horizons barely improve on history
Scored on 2024-08-01 .. 2024-10-31 with everything fitted on 2024-01-01 .. 2024-07-31. **Train at its own later stop** (view B): "delay stays the same" gives R-squared 0.93 at 1 hour, 0.80 at 2, 0.64 at 3 and 0.21 at 6 hours (MAE 2.9, 5.3, 7.5 and 13.9 min); a linear model on current delay alone gives 0.93 / 0.81 / 0.68 / 0.33 (R-squared) but a worse MAE at long horizons (17.2 min at 6 hours); history baselines are far weaker: train x node history R-squared 0.17-0.18 (MAE 12.5-17.1), node x hour history 0.06-0.08 (MAE 13.8-17.5). **Hourly node mean** (view A): persistence is poor beyond 1 hour (R-squared 0.05 at 1 hour, then negative; MAE 9.4-12.5 min) while node x hour history reaches R-squared 0.37 at every horizon (MAE about 8.1-8.2). Descriptive only, the same constants on the fog months Nov-Dec 2024 (12 fog days, mean delay 14.9 against 11.6): persistence R-squared 0.95 at 1 hour and 0.30 at 6 hours, MAE 3.6 to 19.0 min; linear 0.95 to 0.48.
*Figure:* `eda_15_baselines`.
*SIMULATED caveat:* these are the bars for this world only.
*Likely artifact?* **Largely yes.** The very high 1-2 hour persistence R-squared follows directly from finding 2 (delay is carried one-for-one with almost no recovery and no route-dependent shocks); real trains will recover and be re-delayed more, so persistence will likely be weaker. The fog-season performance is descriptive only, and the history baselines suffer from the fog-heavy fit period (Jan-Feb) being scored on summer months.

## Items to verify or keep in mind next
- The conclusions about persistence and carrying (findings 2, 4, 8) depend on the recovery rule; test robustness on the low and high scenarios later (reserved for that).
- Fog is the strongest single factor but exists in only 22 days of the decision window; model selection must follow the rolling-origin rule (mean over both folds, fog reported separately).
- Per-train analyses should exclude 12003 and 12033 until the Shatabdi artifact is understood.
- Definition choices (episode thresholds, 3-arrival rule, 30/15 minutes) were fixed in advance and not tuned; they change the numbers noticeably.
